### Protocol deviation on condition 1, and what it concealed

The pre-registration specifies **30/30** selection controls. A first pass ran **24**: eight donors with
three probes each, which is neither 30 controls nor a sample of the whole set. It reported every arm
passing 24/24.

Re-run correctly at one control per donor across all 30, **S3 at tau = 0.5, 1 and 2 fails condition 1
at 28/30**. The shortcut did not merely under-cover; it hid a real failure of three arms. The
rejection does not depend on it -- condition 3 fails catastrophically for every arm -- but the earlier
"complete pass" was wrong and is retracted here rather than left standing.

### What condition 1 does and does not establish

Passing means a score is **empirically self-consistent on the tested on-panel controls**: counts
generated by a panel pair make that pair its unique optimum there. It does **not** establish that
clump averaging or clipping defines a properly calibrated likelihood or a proper scoring rule. An
earlier draft called them "proper likelihoods"; that is an overclaim and is withdrawn. The defensible
statement is that the arms which pass are self-consistent on-panel and fail on off-panel projection.

### Zero-excess survival, by individual tau

| arm | tau | donors kept exact |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | - | 5/5 |
| S3 | 0.5 | 3/5 |
| S3 | 1 | 3/5 |
| S3 | 2 | 3/5 |
| S3 | 5 | 1/5 |
| S3 | 10 | 1/5 |
| S3 | 20 | 1/5 |
| S2 | inf | **5/5** |

**This is not monotonic in the bound, and an earlier draft said it was.** Within the finite range it
is -- damage worsens as tau grows from 0.5 to 20 -- but removing the bound entirely restores 5/5.
Larger tau means *less* clipping, so a naive reading expects tau=20 to approach S2; instead it is the
worst.

The likely reason is in Phase 0: a handful of clumps carry hundreds of log units each. A loose bound
clips almost nothing EXCEPT those few decisive clumps, removing exactly the evidence that settles the
call, while a tight bound flattens every clump and at least treats them alike. That reading is
consistent with the data here but was not tested, and it is recorded as a hypothesis, not a finding.

## The declared outcome this triggers

From the pre-registration: *"Neither works under noiseless evidence -> the retained feature
representation must change, and no further likelihood parameter is justified."*

**Scoped precisely, what is closed is this:** clump-mean normalization and symmetric clump clipping do
not improve KIV-2 off-panel projection. No further tuning *in this score family* is justified without
a new mechanistic hypothesis -- not another tau, not a median instead of a mean, not a per-locus
variant.

It does **not** prove that every possible likelihood change is invalid, nor that the marker
representation alone must be replaced. Those remain open; this experiment simply does not speak to
them.

The 50-donor holdout stays SEALED. There is nothing to validate, and opening it would burn 50
untouched donors to confirm a failure already visible on the selection set.

## The reason the pre-registration existed

A 9-donor interim, computed while pricing was still running, read:

```
FIRST 9   S1 160256   S2 126966   -20.8%     "S2 improves 3 donors and damages none"
ALL 30    S1 362913   S2 473054   +30.3%
```

**The sign flipped.** Had selection been allowed on partial data, S2 would have been frozen and taken
to the holdout on a 20% improvement that does not exist. The nine were not adversarially chosen --
they were simply the first to finish.

What the full set shows is that S2 changes the call on 13 of 30 donors and the changes are
asymmetric: seven donors get dramatically worse (+5,122 to +38,685 edits) against four that get
better. On 17 donors it selects exactly what production selects.

## What this does not say

- It does not say clump structure is irrelevant. Phase 0 stands: support mismatch is 96.9% of the
  emission's loss and it is concentrated in a handful of clumps. This says that *normalizing or
  bounding the clump contribution* does not convert that observation into a better call.
- It does not say the markers are wrong, or right. That question is still open and needs the
  clump-to-sequence mapping.
- It says nothing about cyp2d6 or gstm1, whose errors are a different bucket.
